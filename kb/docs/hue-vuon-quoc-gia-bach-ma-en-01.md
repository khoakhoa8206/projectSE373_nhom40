# Bạch Mã National Park

## Overview
Bạch Mã National Park is a protected area in central Vietnam near Huế and Đà Nẵng. It covers 220 km², divided into a strictly protected core, an administrative area and a buffer zone.

## Protection history
The area was first protected as forest reserves in 1937, declared a protected area by the South Vietnamese government in 1962 and established as a national park in 1986.

## Landscape and climate
The park lies in the Annamite Mountains and is among the wettest places in Vietnam; its mountains are mostly steep granite. It sits on the biogeographical boundary between northern and southern Vietnam, with habitats from the coast to high mountains. The cooler climate of the summit area, about 1,250 m above sea level, makes it a popular summer retreat.

## Wildlife
The main vegetation is moist evergreen and montane forest. The park protects endemic birds such as the Vietnamese crested argus, Annam partridge and Edwards's pheasant, and BirdLife International lists it as an Important Bird Area. Historically it was home to Asian elephants, white-cheeked gibbons and red-shanked douc langurs. Defoliants such as Agent Orange damaged its forests during the Vietnam War.

## French hill station
In 1932 the French engineer Girard chose the summit as a hill station for Huế's colonial administration. By 1937 there were 139 villas and hotels, with a post office, market and hospital, reached by a steep 19-km road, and the site was called the “Dalat of central Vietnam”. After fighting in the early 1950s the villas were abandoned; only a few stone walls remain.
