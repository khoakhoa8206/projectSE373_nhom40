# Imperial Academy (Quốc Tử Giám), Huế

## Overview
The Imperial Academy (Quốc Tử Giám) was the national academy of the Nguyễn dynasty. It stands on the right side of the Imperial City of Huế.

## Founding
After unifying Vietnam and moving the capital from Hanoi to Huế, Emperor Gia Long had a new Confucian academy built in 1803 to replace the Lê dynasty's Quốc Tử Giám. The first academy, Đốc Học đường, was a small group of buildings in An Ninh Thượng village (Hương Trà district), about 5 km from Huế, beside a Temple of Literature.

## Minh Mạng and Tự Đức
In March 1820 Emperor Minh Mạng renamed it Quốc Tử Giám, rebuilt and expanded it, and added Di Luân Palace with one teaching hall, two teaching rooms and 19 classrooms. Emperor Tự Đức enlarged the academy further, built a surrounding wall and erected a stone stele with his commandments for students.

## Relocation and closure
A hurricane badly damaged the academy in 1904 and it was repaired soon after. In 1908, under Emperor Duy Tân, the academy moved inside the citadel to its present site; almost all buildings were rebuilt there except Di Luân Palace. It closed permanently in 1945, after the fall of the Nguyễn dynasty.
