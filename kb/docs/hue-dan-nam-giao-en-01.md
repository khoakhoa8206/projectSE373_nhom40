# Nam Giao Esplanade (Esplanade of Sacrifice to the Heaven and Earth)

## Overview
The Nam Giao Esplanade is the Nguyễn dynasty's altar for sacrifices to Heaven and Earth, south of Huế, and part of the Complex of Huế Monuments. According to the source it was established in 1803 under Emperor Gia Long.

## Structure
The altar has three levels: the first, where the presiding officiant washes his hands; the second, where offerings are presented; and the third, which holds the main altars.

## The Nam Giao rite under the Nguyễn
Rituals of offering to Heaven and Earth existed before the Nguyễn dynasty. Nguyễn emperors performed the rite until 1945, after a three-day fast at Trai Cung Palace. Under the Nguyễn it was held every three years on a date chosen by the royal astronomers. Offerings included a pig, a goat and a water buffalo sacrificed the day before, with wine, silk and gems brought from provinces across the empire.

## After 1945 and today
After the monarchy ended in 1945 the altar fell into disuse and deteriorated, and it has been preserved and restored in recent decades. A late-night re-enactment of the ceremony is now held as part of the Huế Festival, presided over by city leaders and open to the public.
