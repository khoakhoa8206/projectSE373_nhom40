# Báo Quốc Pagoda

## Overview
Báo Quốc Pagoda stands on Báo Quốc Street in Phường Đúc ward, on the south side of the Perfume River about 1 km west of central Huế, on a small hill called Hàm Long; a spring from the hilltop runs through the grounds. Under the Nguyễn dynasty it was one of Huế's three national temples.

## Founding
The Chinese-born Zen master Thích Giác Phong built the temple in 1670, under the Nguyễn lord Nguyễn Phúc Tần, with the first name Hàm Long Sơn Thiên Thọ Tự. In 1747 Lord Nguyễn Phúc Khoát presented a plaque reading “Sắc Tứ Báo Quốc Tự”.

## Under the Nguyễn emperors
In 1808 Empress Hiếu Khương, wife of Gia Long, funded a triple gate, a large bell and a gong, and the temple was renamed Thiên Thọ. In 1824 Emperor Minh Mạng visited and restored the name Báo Quốc; in 1830 he celebrated his 40th birthday there. From 1858 Emperor Tự Đức led renovations funded by the royal family, continuing until the end of the century.

## Buddhist education
In the 1930s the temple became a centre of revived Buddhist education: a Buddhist school opened in 1935 and a monastery for training monks was founded in 1940; the monastery still operates. In 1959 Hàm Long Primary School opened on the grounds; it was later renamed Hàm Long Bodhi School and closed in 1975.

## Architecture
The temple covers about 2 hectares; a triple gate leads to a broad, planted courtyard. On the left are stupas of the patriarchs; the oldest, for Thích Giác Phong, was completed in 1714 and is 3.3 m tall. The main hall has four dragon-decorated pillars; the largest statue is a triple Buddha image, with a small stupa holding relics of Gautama Buddha in front.
