# Nhã nhạc – Huế court music

## Overview
Nhã nhạc (雅樂, often rendered “elegant music”) is Vietnamese court music, performed from the Trần dynasty (13th century) to the Nguyễn dynasty.

## Under the Nguyễn
Nhã nhạc reached its fullest development under the Nguyễn dynasty, when it was standardised, made the official court music and placed at the centre of palace rituals. The court also adopted some elements of the music of Champa.

## Occasions
It was performed at annual ceremonies such as anniversaries and religious holidays, and at special events such as coronations, funerals and official receptions, by highly trained musicians accompanied by court dances and elaborate costumes.

## Instruments
Instruments include the kèn bầu (conical oboe), đàn tỳ bà (four-string pear-shaped lute), đàn nguyệt (two-string moon lute), đàn tam (three-string fretless lute with a snakeskin body), đàn nhị (two-string fiddle), sáo trúc (bamboo flute), drums and other percussion.

## UNESCO
In 2003 “Nhã nhạc of the Huế court” was proclaimed a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity by UNESCO. It is still performed in Huế and is being preserved.
