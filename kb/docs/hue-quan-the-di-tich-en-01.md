# Complex of Huế Monuments

## Overview
The Complex of Huế Monuments is a group of historic sites in the city of Huế, central Vietnam. Huế became the capital of a newly unified Vietnam in 1802 under Emperor Gia Long and served as the political, cultural and religious centre of the Nguyễn empire.

## Components
The complex includes the Citadel (Kinh thành), the Imperial City (Hoàng thành) and the Purple Forbidden City (Tử Cấm Thành); the royal tombs of Gia Long, Minh Mạng, Thiệu Trị, Tự Đức, Dục Đức, Đồng Khánh and Khải Định; and a number of temples, pagodas and other spiritual sites.

## UNESCO listing
The complex was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993, at the 17th session of the World Heritage Committee, under cultural criterion (iv). Its UNESCO reference number is 678.

## Design principles
The layout follows geomancy (phong thủy) and the theory of the Five Elements, which links the five cardinal points, five elements and five colours. The plan is oriented along a north–south axis and organised in three concentric enclosures. Pairing the river with nearby hills is a distinctive feature of the site. The architecture blends earlier Vietnamese traditions with Chinese fine arts and some Western influences introduced by French architects who worked for Gia Long.
