# Imperial City of Huế (Hoàng thành)

## Overview
The Imperial City (Hoàng thành) lies inside the walled Citadel (Kinh thành) of Huế. It was modelled on the Forbidden City in Beijing but faces southeast toward the Perfume River (Hương River) rather than due south. Behind the Thái Hòa throne hall lies the Purple Forbidden City (Tử Cấm Thành), the private residence of the royal family.

## Construction
Construction of the imperial palace began in 1804 under Emperor Gia Long. The complex was remodelled and enlarged to its present scale in 1833 under Emperor Minh Mạng. The Imperial City is roughly square, with sides of about 600 m and walls about 4 m high.

## Gates
The Meridian Gate (Ngọ Môn) on the south is the largest gate and was used by the emperor. The other gates are Hiển Nhơn Gate on the east, Chương Đức Gate on the west and Hòa Bình Gate on the north.

## Main structures
- **Thái Hòa Palace:** the main ceremonial hall, begun in 1805 and rebuilt and moved in 1833. All 13 Nguyễn emperors, from Gia Long to Bảo Đại, were crowned here.
- **Purple Forbidden City:** built in 1804 under the name Cung thành and renamed in 1822.
- **Thế Miếu:** the dynastic temple; in front of it stand the Nine Dynastic Urns, cast between 1835 and 1837 under Minh Mạng.
- **Hiển Lâm Pavilion:** built in 1821–1822; at 17 m it is the tallest structure in the Imperial City.
- **Triệu Tổ Miếu and Hưng Miếu:** temples built in 1804.

## Events of the 20th century
Emperor Bảo Đại abdicated at the Imperial City on 30 August 1945. In February 1947 the Việt Minh seized the citadel; the French counter-attack led to a six-week battle in which the core of the complex, including the Imperial Palace, was burned. During the Tết Offensive of 1968, which began on 31 January, heavy fighting caused further damage: of about 160 buildings, only 10 major sites remained.

## Restoration and heritage status
The surviving buildings are being restored. Tây Khuyết Đài was rebuilt in 2012, Nhật Thành Pavilion was reconstructed in 2018 and the restoration of Đông Khuyết Đài was completed in November 2023. The Imperial City is part of the Complex of Huế Monuments, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.
