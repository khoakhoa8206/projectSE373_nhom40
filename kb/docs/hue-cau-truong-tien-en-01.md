# Trường Tiền Bridge

## Overview
Trường Tiền Bridge crosses the Perfume River in central Huế and carries pedestrians and vehicles. It is a steel Gothic-style structure 403 m long and 5.45 m high; according to the source, its northern end is in Đông Ba ward and its southern end in Phú Hội ward.

## Construction
In 1897 the French Resident Superior of Annam chose the firm Schneider et Cie and the Letellier team to design and build it, at an estimated cost of 732,456 francs. Work began in May 1899 and the bridge was inaugurated on 18 December 1900. It has six arches of comb-shaped steel girders, each spanning 67 m, with a 6 m wide deck.

## Names
Besides Trường Tiền, it has been called Thành Thái Bridge, Mống Bridge, Nguyễn Hoàng Bridge and Clémenceau Bridge. In 1907, after the French exiled Emperor Thành Thái, the colonial government renamed it after Georges Clemenceau, French Prime Minister during the First World War.

## Repairs and widening
The 1904 “Year of the Dragon” flood badly damaged the bridge; it reopened in 1906 with a concrete road surface instead of wooden planks. The Eiffel company was later hired to replace damaged iron beams, lay a reinforced-concrete deck, add two 1.95 m sidewalks and apply anti-rust paint, at a cost of 435,000 francs. A further widening began on 20 June 1937 under engineer Martin André and was completed in November 1939.

## War and restoration
The bridge collapsed during fighting in 1968 and suffered repeated damage and temporary repairs. A long-term rebuilding ran from 1991 to 1995: the side pedestrian balconies were removed, the roadway narrowed to fit two extra bracing rails, and the paint changed from silver to grey. Permanent coloured lights were installed in 2002 for a festival.
