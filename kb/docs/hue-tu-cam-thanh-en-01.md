# Purple Forbidden City (Huế)

## Overview
The Purple Forbidden City (Tử Cấm Thành) lies behind Thái Hòa Palace inside the Imperial City of Huế. It held the emperor's residence and offices, including Càn Thành Hall.

## History
It was built in 1804, the 3rd year of Emperor Gia Long's reign, under the name Cung thành, and was renamed the Purple Forbidden City in 1822 after being enlarged.

## Buildings
Đại Cung Môn, built in 1833, has three doors, the central one reserved for the emperor. Cần Chánh Hall, built in 1804 and rebuilt in 1827, 1850 and 1899, hosted the daily court and court meetings on the 5th, 10th, 20th and 25th days of each lunar month. Càn Thành Hall was built in 1811 as Trung Hòa Điện and renamed in 1839.

## Destruction and restoration
Most buildings were destroyed or left in ruins during the Indochina Wars: Đại Cung Môn and Cần Chánh Hall were burned in February 1947, Càn Thành Hall was destroyed in February 1947 and Đông Các in 1947. Tả Vu and Hữu Vu (built in 1819) were restored in 1977, 1986 and 1997–1999. Restoration is ongoing.
