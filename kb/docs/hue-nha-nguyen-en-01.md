# Nguyễn dynasty – overview

## Overview
The Nguyễn dynasty ruled Vietnam from 1802 until Emperor Bảo Đại's abdication on 25 August 1945, with its capital at Phú Xuân, now part of Huế. The state was called Việt Nam, a name approved by the Qing court in 1804, and from 1839 Đại Nam.

## Founding
Nguyễn Phúc Ánh was crowned in Huế on 1 June 1802 with the era name Gia Long, after defeating the Tây Sơn dynasty, and captured Hanoi on 20 July 1802. He built a Confucian bureaucracy and issued the Gia Long Code in 1812.

## Minh Mạng
Emperor Minh Mạng (reigned 1820–1841) centralised power through a provincial bureaucracy, turned the Cham principality of Thuận Thành into a province in 1832 and annexed Cambodia in 1834.

## French conquest
A Franco-Spanish force attacked Đà Nẵng in September 1858. The Treaty of Saigon (June 1862) ceded three southern provinces, and a further treaty in 1874 completed the annexation of Cochinchina. The Harmand Convention (1883) and the Protectorate Treaty of 6 June 1884 made the Nguyễn a vassal monarchy, and the Treaty of Tientsin (9 June 1885) recognised French dominion.

## End of the dynasty
On 9 March 1945 Japan overthrew the French administration. Bảo Đại proclaimed independence on 11 March and abdicated on 25 August 1945 after the August Revolution, ending 143 years of Nguyễn rule.
