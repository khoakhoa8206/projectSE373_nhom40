# Meridian Gate (Ngọ Môn), Huế

## Overview
The Meridian Gate (Ngọ Môn, 午門), also called the South Gate, is the main gate of the Imperial City inside the Huế Citadel. It was built in 1833, in the 14th year of the reign of Emperor Minh Mạng.

## History
Before the gate was built, the site was occupied by Nam Khuyết Đài, a gate built early in the reign of Emperor Gia Long. Nam Khuyết Đài had a main keep called Càn Nguyên Điện and two side gates, Tả Đoan Môn and Hữu Đoan Môn. Minh Mạng had it removed when he reorganised the layout of the Imperial City. The Meridian Gate survived the large-scale destruction of the Vietnam War.

## Design and symbolism
According to the Book of Changes, the south is the direction from which the emperor "listens to the world and rules wisely", which is why the main gate faces south. The gate was modelled on the Meridian Gate of the Forbidden City in Beijing: a central section is flanked by two protruding wings that represent watchtowers (que towers).

## Structure
The lower part is a fortress-like base of stone and brick; the upper part is a more elaborate, palace-like structure. At ground level there are five entrances. The central entrance was reserved for the monarch. The two slightly smaller entrances on either side were for mandarins, soldiers and horses, and the two small arched entrances further out were for everyone else.

## Five Phoenix Pavilion
The upper level holds the Five Phoenix Pavilion (Lầu Ngũ Phụng). From its main hall the emperor watched troop parades and ceremonies and received the homage of his subjects. The roof is covered with yellow glazed tiles decorated with animal figures meant to ward off evil, and the two side pavilions were reserved for members of the court.
