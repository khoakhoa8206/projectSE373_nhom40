# Bún bò Huế

## Overview
Bún bò Huế is a rice noodle soup with sliced beef, chả lụa (pork roll) and sometimes pork knuckle. It comes from Huế, whose cooking is tied to the former royal court; in Huế itself it is simply called bún bò.

## Origins
Its invention is dated to 1585–1602, when the Nguyễn lords ruled Huế. One theory says it was first served in the royal kitchens before spreading to street stalls and homes; another credits a young woman known as Cô Bún with perfecting noodle-making in Vân Cù village, which has made noodles for over 400 years.

## Broth
The broth simmers beef bones and beef shank with lemongrass and is seasoned with fermented shrimp sauce and sugar; chili oil is added later. Lemongrass and shrimp paste dominate, giving a mix of spicy, sour, sweet, salty and savoury flavours.

## Noodles and toppings
The noodles are thick and cylindrical. Typical toppings are thin slices of marinated boiled beef shank, oxtail and pork knuckle; some versions add pig blood curd.

## How it is served
It comes with lime wedges, cilantro, green onion, raw onion, chili sauce and thinly sliced banana blossom, plus herbs such as mint, basil, perilla, rau răm and ngò gai. In Huế it is eaten in the morning as a main meal.
