# Diệu Đế Pagoda

## Overview
Diệu Đế Pagoda in Huế is named after the Four Noble Truths (Tứ Diệu Đế). Its entrance is on the bank of the Đông Ba canal and faces south-west; across the canal is the Đông Ba gate of the Huế Citadel.

## National pagoda
The pagoda was built during the reign of Emperor Thiệu Trị in the 19th century. He declared it a national pagoda, one of three in Huế with this status, under the direct patronage of the Nguyễn dynasty.

## Architecture
Four low towers stand around the complex: one on each side of the main gate and two flanking the sanctuary; they hold bells and a stele. The main hall has a statue of Gautama Buddha flanked by the Bodhisattvas Phổ Hiền (Samantabhadra) and Văn Thù Sư Lợi (Manjushri).

## 20th century
During the 1963 Buddhist crisis, on the night of 21 August 1963 the Special Forces of Ngô Đình Diệm's government raided pagodas across the country; outside Diệu Đế, crowds defended the bridge for about five hours until troops took it at dawn. After Diệm was deposed the pagoda became a centre of Buddhist and student anti-war protests, and in 1966 police and army forces stormed it and arrested many monks and protesters.
