# Tomb of Khải Định (Ứng Lăng)

## Overview
The Tomb of Khải Định, officially Ứng Lăng, stands on Châu Chữ mountain near Huế. It is part of the Complex of Huế Monuments, inscribed by UNESCO in 1993, and covers an area of 18.19 ha.

## Construction
Work began on 4 September 1920. Emperor Khải Định died in 1925 and the tomb was completed in 1931 under his son Bảo Đại, after 11 years of construction.

## Architecture
The tomb blends Vietnamese architecture with Western styles, including Baroque, Gothic and Neoclassical elements. Unlike earlier royal tombs it is built largely of concrete, steel, iron and slate rather than wood or brick, and its walls and interiors are decorated with glass and porcelain.

## Approach
A grand staircase leads to a triple-arched gateway on the first terrace. A salutation court lined with two rows of stone mandarins, attendants and animals leads to a two-tiered octagonal stele pavilion.

## Thiên Định Palace
The main building, Thiên Định Palace, consists of five connected halls, the number five relating to the five elements. The interior ceiling of nine dragons was originally painted by the royal painter Phan Văn Tánh. The central altar room is called Khải Thành Palace. A life-size bronze statue of Khải Định, 160 cm tall, shows the emperor in military-style regalia; in the rear room a seated bronze statue in imperial dress, cast in Marseille, stands beside his grave and altar.
