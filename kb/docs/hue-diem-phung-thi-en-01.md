# Điềm Phùng Thị – sculptor and her art centre in Huế

## Overview
Điềm Phùng Thị (born Phùng Thị Cúc on 18 August 1920 in Huế, died in Huế on 28 January 2002) was a Vietnamese sculptor described as one of the masters of Vietnamese modern art. Much of her work was donated to the city of Huế and is shown at the Điềm Phùng Thị Art Museum.

## Early life
She studied dentistry at Hanoi Medical University and in 1946 was one of its first women graduates. After about two years in the First Indochina War she was taken to France for treatment in 1948 after suffering paralysis; she stayed and earned a doctorate in dental surgery in 1954.

## Becoming a sculptor
She gave up dentistry for art school in Paris, began sculpting around 1959, when she was nearly 40, and studied under the sculptor Antoniucci Volti from 1961 to 1963. Within a decade she moved from figurative work to abstraction.

## The seven modules
Her signature method used seven shapes she called the “seven modules”, which could be combined in seemingly endless ways. She worked in terracotta, stone, metal, wood, lacquer, polyester and even scraps of B-52 bombs.

## Exhibitions and return to Huế
Her first Paris exhibition was held in 1966 at the Bernheim-Jeune gallery, and her 1978 exhibition in Vietnam is considered one of the country's first shows of abstract art. In 1992 she returned to Vietnam and settled in her hometown of Huế.
