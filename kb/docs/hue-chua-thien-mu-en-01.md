# Thiên Mụ Pagoda

## Overview
Thiên Mụ Pagoda, also called Linh Mụ, stands on Hà Khê hill on the northern bank of the Perfume River, about 5 km from the Huế Citadel. Its seven-storey Phước Duyên tower is regarded as the unofficial symbol of the city.

## Founding and legend
Nguyễn Hoàng, governor of Thuận Hóa, ordered the pagoda built in 1601. According to the royal annals, a local legend told of an old lady called Thiên Mụ ("heavenly lady"), dressed in red and blue, who foretold that a lord would build a pagoda on the hill to pray for the country's prosperity. On hearing this, Nguyễn Hoàng ordered the pagoda built there.

## Expansion
Nguyễn Phúc Tần undertook major construction in 1665. In 1695 the Chinese Zen master Thích Đại Sán arrived and served as abbot until 1696. In 1710 Nguyễn Phúc Chu had a great bell cast, weighing 3,285 kg and said to be audible 10 km away, and in 1714 he oversaw the largest expansion in the pagoda's history, adding gates, shrines, halls, towers and quarters for monks.

## Phước Duyên tower
In 1844 Emperor Thiệu Trị built the Từ Nhân tower, now called Phước Duyên. The octagonal brick tower has seven storeys, each dedicated to a different Buddha, and is 21 m tall.

## Damage and restoration
A cyclone badly damaged the pagoda in 1904. Emperor Thành Thái authorised its reconstruction in 1907, although it never regained its former grandeur.

## Notable objects
The grounds hold a large marble turtle, a symbol of longevity, and steles carrying poems by Thiệu Trị. The main hall houses a statue of Maitreya. The pagoda also keeps the Austin car in which the monk Thích Quảng Đức was driven to Saigon in 1963, where he burned himself to death in protest against the Ngô Đình Diệm government.
