# Tomb of Tự Đức (Khiêm Lăng)

## Overview
The Tomb of Tự Đức, officially Khiêm Lăng (謙陵), is part of the Complex of Huế Monuments. It was built between 1864 and 1867.

## Construction
Building the tomb required heavy corvée labour and extra taxes, which led to an unsuccessful coup against Emperor Tự Đức in 1866.

## Layout
The site is divided into a temple area and a tomb area. The palace buildings served as Tự Đức's residence for the rest of his life. Named structures include Xung Khiêm Pavilion, Dũ Khiêm Pavilion, Khiêm Cung Gate, Lương Khiêm Palace and Minh Khiêm Theatre. A lake with a small island lies in the grounds; boats moored at Dũ Khiêm Pavilion, from where visitors walked west into the palace area.

## The stele
The stele, described as the largest of its kind in Vietnam, was brought from a quarry more than 500 km away, a journey that took four years. It carries an account of his reign that Tự Đức wrote himself, which he regarded as a bad omen. It stands in a pavilion just east of the emperor's tomb.

## Burial
Tự Đức is not actually buried at the tomb; his real burial place remains hidden somewhere in Huế. His adopted son and successor, Kiến Phúc, is buried in a small corner of the grounds, and the tomb of Empress Lệ Thiên Anh lies between the tombs of Tự Đức and Kiến Phúc.
